Test rigs, drivetrains, and the analysis behind them.

Mechanical engineering at the University of Florida. Four projects, and what I did on each.

Regenerative dynamometer for in-hub motor testing

Gator Motorsports — University of Florida

A test stand for in-hub motors. Two motors face each other through a single torque transducer, so one drives while the other loads it and the energy recirculates instead of being burned off in a brake.

  • Sized the stand and laid out the back-to-back architecture.
  • Moved the adapter plate from aluminium to steel, then ran a design study to settle its diameter and thickness.
  • Verified the plate to a 2x safety factor at 500 N·m in ANSYS.
  • Ran the thermal and static studies on the housing in SolidWorks Simulation.
  • Extended a MATLAB solver to rank whole driveline builds by first natural frequency, so components were chosen together rather than one at a time.

Aerial & ground platform redesign

Docking Robotics

An aerial platform and the ground platform it lands on. The two have to dock reliably, which constrains both the airframe and the cradle that receives it.

  • Redesigned the motor arms: longer, to push the props away from the centre of mass for stability.
  • Raised them so the camera module stops blocking lift and gets the clearance it needs.
  • Tuned infill and cutout pattern together for strength-to-weight, since the arms are 3D printed.
  • Designed the landing cradle that lets the aerial platform dock with the ground platform.

Parametric modeling of the in-hub gear set

Gator Motorsports — University of Florida

The planetary gear set inside the in-hub motor. Tooth counts kept moving while the set was being sized, so the model had to be re-driven rather than redrawn each time.

  • Built the gear geometry in Siemens NX as an expression-driven model: inputs hold numbers, everything else is a formula.
  • Took over an existing MATLAB gear search and bounded it to the corner where feasible sets actually land, which made it quick enough to iterate on.
  • Added a hunting-ratio check, so wear spreads across every tooth instead of repeating the same pairings.

Autonomous abort sequencing — a five-state fail-safe controller

University of Florida

A five-state fail-safe controller for an autonomous vehicle, built as the end-of-term project for an electrical engineering class.

  • Wrote the five-state machine and the abort sequencing it drives.
  • Exercised it on a bench by injecting the lost-link, thermal and geofence faults it exists to catch.
  • Logged all eight subsystems through the sequence.
