Regenerative dynamometer for in-hub motor testing

Gator Motorsports — University of Florida

A test stand for in-hub motors. Two motors face each other through a single torque transducer, so one drives while the other loads it and the energy recirculates instead of being burned off in a brake.

What I did

  • Sized the stand and laid out the back-to-back architecture.
  • Moved the adapter plate from aluminium to steel, then ran a design study to settle its diameter and thickness.
  • Verified the plate to a 2x safety factor at 500 N·m in ANSYS, and ran the thermal case.
  • Extended a MATLAB solver to rank whole driveline builds by first natural frequency, so components were chosen together rather than one at a time.

The work

The stand the rest of this work sizes. Facing two in-hub motors back-to-back through one transducer lets the rig load a motor with another motor rather than a brake, which is what makes it regenerative.

The plate moved from aluminium to steel for a better balance of stiffness against inertia. This study then settled the two dimensions that choice leaves open: outside diameter and base thickness. Displacement, not mass, was the binding constraint.

Verification rather than exploration. With the dimensions settled, this confirmed the plate carries the 500 N·m load case to a 2× safety factor before anything was cut.

Whatever temperature the housing settles at is what the surrounding parts and seals have to tolerate, and it sets the thermal growth the structural case has to absorb. Steady state lands between 120 and 127 degrees Celsius, hottest at the inner groove where the heat goes in and coolest at the lugs that carry it back out.

% --- System equivalent stiffness (series) ---
inv_k = (1/k_a1) + (1/k_c1) + (1/k_t) + (1/k_c2) + (1/k_a2);
k_sys = 1 / inv_k;

% --- System total inertia (sum) ---
j_sys = j_a1 + j_c1 + j_t + j_c2 + j_a2;

% --- Natural Frequency (Hz) ---
fn = (1 / (2*pi)) * sqrt(k_sys / j_sys);

Stiffness and inertia anywhere in the driveline contaminate the transient torque reading, so components could not be chosen one at a time. Ranking whole builds on first natural frequency folds both into a single number. That is why rank 2 loses despite being 20% stiffer than rank 1: it carries 75% more inertia. Pushing that first mode high enough is what lets the rig run dynamic tests at all.

Parametric modeling of the in-hub gear set

Gator Motorsports — University of Florida

The planetary gear set inside the in-hub motor. Tooth counts kept moving while the set was being sized, so the model had to be re-driven rather than redrawn each time.

What I did

  • Built the gear geometry in Siemens NX as an expression-driven model: inputs hold numbers, everything else is a formula.
  • Took over an existing MATLAB gear search and bounded it to the corner where feasible sets actually land, which made it quick enough to iterate on.
  • Added a hunting-ratio check, so wear spreads across every tooth instead of repeating the same pairings.

The work

The tooth counts were still moving while the set was being sized, and a gear redrawn by hand every time they change is a gear drawn wrong eventually. Inputs hold numbers, every other row is a formula referencing them, so a new set gets typed in rather than modelled again.

What fed those numbers. The search itself was not mine. It was already sweeping every gear combination against AGMA bending and contact limits, but slowly enough to be awkward to iterate on. Feasible sets only ever land where the ring gear is near its package limit and the sun near its minimum, so the sweep was bounded to that corner rather than the whole space. The hunting-ratio check went in alongside it: coprime meshes let every tooth eventually meet every other, so wear spreads instead of repeating the same pairings.

Structural validation of the in-hub motor housing

Gator Motorsports — University of Florida

The in-hub motor housing, checked in SolidWorks Simulation before committing to it.

What I did

  • Ran the static case on displacement rather than stress, since it is stiffness that keeps the bore and sealing faces true.
  • Peak movement came out at 0.20 mm, out at the mounting lugs.

The work

Stiffness, not strength, is what decides whether the bore and the sealing faces stay true once the motor is loaded, so the study was read on displacement. Peak movement is 0.20 mm and it sits out at the mounting lugs, while the inner land holds near 0.12 mm.
